What is activation steering in language models?

29 May 2026

Recently I have come across the concept of activation steering in language models1, and I find the idea quite elegant. It offers a more principled approach to model alignment than “prompt engineering”, and one more cost effective than fine-tuning a billion-parameter model (realistically only for big tech and deep pockets... finance). The idea, although hidden behind the usual superfluous jargon of any ML paper, is quite simple. Suppose you want to align a text with a sentiment (talk in a happy tone) or a topic (change the location of a place), or remove toxicity from text. You would simply pick (yes, you have to think) “opposing pairs” of free text, such as the canonical example of “love” and “hate”, do a full forward pass on your favourite LM, and then take the difference of layer $\ell$’s activations for both free texts to find the “love” − “hate” vector. The idea is that the difference removes noise, positional artefacts, and/or the simple encoding that tells us this is a word, and leaves the direction in which these two free texts differ: the axis we care about. This is the steering vector. What is nice is that we can evaluate this for any layer (which is a hyperparameter to be tuned), and move texts in the direction we want for free (no backward pass) and in a systematic way. It is effectively analogous to the word2vec embedding example, king - man + woman = queen: where there we can move in the space of words, here we can move beyond words and steer free text in any direction we want.

1 A. M. Turner, et al. “Steering Language Models with Activation Engineering.” arXiv preprint arXiv:2308.10248 (2023).
