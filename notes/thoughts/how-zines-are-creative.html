How zines are saving my creativity

14 September 2026

Zines are defined as "a noncommercial often homemade or online publication usually devoted to specialized and often unconventional subject matter,"1 usually created to display ones interests or hobbies. They grew out of the Great Depression in America, where resources were scarce and the format let creative people express themselves independently. I came across the website2 which lets you easily put together your pictures, then print and fold an A4 sheet of paper in such a way that you create a little booklet to preserve a memory, designed however you want.

This vision appeals to me. Developing a roll of film costs upwards of £20 (not including the cost of the film itself), and printing individual photos means paper and ink, which have only become more expensive. Zines let you show your interest in physical media without breaking the bank, and in the process let you see your photos in a different way. So this felt like a natural way to display my photography, a cheap alternative to printing the photos out, and to create little booklets of my travels, days and moments that I can look back on. More importantly, this process lets me think about composition and storytelling through my pictures, which is something I value and didn't have the tools to do before.

Here are some that I have designed. These are a first step toward showing my photography in a different medium, one where I hope they become accessible and fun to look back on. Quality, definition and expensive apparatus aren't needed to evoke a memory. Perhaps some time, dedication and a bit of love in creating these little zines can be enough.
