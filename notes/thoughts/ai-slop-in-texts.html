degradation in communication

24 june 2026

i came across two articles; "no slop grenade"1 and the other by josh moody2, where they raise their frustrations about ai-generated text communication channels and put forward methods (albeit silly) to tackle ai usage, like using emoticons so the message isn't taken seriously, or simply sharing the former article to call out the author. josh defines selfish use of llms as a "situation in which someone uses an llm to save their own time at the expense of other people's time, resulting in a net productivity loss." while i do not agree that we should shame people for how they express themselves i do agree with the sentiment that ai-generated text is damaging communication and it made me reflect on my own expirences.

currently i am working on a project which has come to a standstill. after trying to solve a problem by hand, provide a solution and write it up in less than 4 pages so my collaborators can work off it and give feedback, in return i get an ai-generated wall of text giving me "feedback" about the work. some of the ideas weren't present in the document or repo, suggesting that the sender didn't read either the document or the message they sent. since this is a side project it is frustrating but i can talk to the person directly. however, this is not only degrading research and collaboration but communication more generally.

this isn't the first time this has happened. in normal communication channels like teams, email and slack at work, it is extremely widespread. traditionally these mediums were quick and easily digestible, now plagued with walls of text where the sender hasn't even read or put thought into it. i understand using this for tedious tasks like responding to a landlord with correct grammar or punctuation, or using it to make sure your work is free of grammatical errors. but using this to answer simple questions, or in the case of a discussion with a peer, makes little sense. we outsource thinking to an llm and in turn expect the reader to decipher what is being said, when the sender never had any intention of thinking about the problem. this is made more evident in simple tasks: if someone asks you a yes/no question or what you'd personally prefer, we do not need to airbrush our responses. however, this raises serious questions about how we use ai, and to what extent. using it in these channels damages communication, but what about when it is used to review journal papers? how are people using these tools? are they pushing back on the llms, or accepting their outputs as truth?
