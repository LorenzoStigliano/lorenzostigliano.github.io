degradation in communication: ai-generated walls of text

24 june 2026

i came across the article "how to passive-aggressively shame people who use llms selfishly"1 by josh moody, where he defines selfish use of llms as a "situation in which someone uses an llm to save their own time at the expense of other people's time, resulting in a net productivity loss." and i have to say i do agree with the sentiment and made me reflect on my own time in academia to date.

currently i am working on project which has come to a stand still. after trying to solve a problem by hand, provide a solution, run experiments and write it up in less than 4 pages such that my collaborators can work off of it and give feedback. in return i get an ai-generated wall of text giving me "feedback" about the work. many of the ideas not present in the document or repo, suggesting that the sender didn't read either the document or message they sent. a lack of critical thinking or engangement. clearly this is not only degrading research and collaboration but communication more generally. time would have been better spent if they simply said that they didn't want to work on the project.

this isnt the first time this has happened, in normal communcation channels like teams, email and slack to work, it is extremley wide spread. traditionally these mediums where quick, easily digestible now plagued with walls of texts where the sender hasnt even read or put thought into it. i understand using this (to a certain extent) for tedious tasks like responding to a landlord with correct grammar or punctuation or using this to make sure your work is free of gramatical errors or flaws. but using this to answer simple questions or in the case of an discussion with a peer this makes no sense. we out source thinking to a llm and in turn expect the reader to decipher what is being said, the sender never had intentions of thinking about the problem. this is made more evident in simple tasks if some one asks you a yes/no question or a personal preference of what you want to do, we do not need to airbrush our responses to be "polite" or "correct".

i am not the only one who is also getting frustrated at this phenomenon. in both josh moody's article and "no slop grenade"2 article they raise thier frustrations and put forward retailations to selfish ai usage, using emoticons to not take these message seriously or simply sharing the later article to call out the author of the ai-generated wall of text to perhaps reflect and rewrite the mesage without the use of ai?
