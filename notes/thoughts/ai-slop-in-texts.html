degradation of digital communication

24 june 2026

i came across two articles; "no slop grenade"1 and the other by josh moody2, where they raise their frustrations about ai-generated text in everyday communication channels and put forward methods (albeit silly) to tackle ai usage, like using emoticons so the message isn't taken seriously, or simply sharing the former article to call out the author. josh defines selfish use of llms as a "situation in which someone uses an llm to save their own time at the expense of other people's time, resulting in a net productivity loss." while i do not agree that we should shame people for how they express themselves i do agree with the sentiment that ai-generated text is damaging communication and it made me reflect on my own expirences.

in normal communication channels like teams, email and slack at work, it is extremely widespread. traditionally these mediums were quick and easily digestible, now plagued with walls of text where the sender hasn't even read or put thought into it. i understand using this for tedious tasks like responding to a landlord with correct grammar or punctuation, or using it to make sure your work is free of grammatical errors. but using this to answer simple questions, or in the case of a discussion with a peer, makes little sense. we outsource thinking to an llm and in turn expect the reader to decipher what is being said, when the sender never had any intention of thinking about the problem. this is made more evident in simple tasks: if someone asks you a yes/no question or what you'd personally prefer, we do not need to airbrush our responses.

clearly, using llm in digital channels damages communication. as ai is being acepted and used throughout the same concerns and issues can extend to any form of communication such as academic articles or literary works. raising questions about how we use ai, and to what extent. what happens when ai is used to review journal papers? how are people using these tools? are they pushing back on the llms, or accepting their outputs as truth?
